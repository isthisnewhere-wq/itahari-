Four ways to enjoy momo.

Steam, jhol, kothey, and chilli: a practical guide to choosing a momo style.

Momo is a filled dumpling, and its preparation changes how the dish eats. Bahattar Cafe lists four styles, each with Veg, Chicken, or Pork options. The prices below are the supplied menu prices.

Steam: a simple starting point

Steam Momo is listed at Rs. 155. Steaming cooks dumplings without a fried exterior, making this a useful starting point when you want to focus on the filling and wrapper. Ask what sauce accompanies the café’s serving.

Jhol: dumplings with a soupy sauce

Jhol Momo costs Rs. 185. Jhol generally refers to a liquid or soupy sauce around the dumplings. The particular ingredients and level of heat vary, so check with the team if you prefer milder food or have allergies.

Deep Fry or Kothey: choose texture

The menu groups Deep Fry or Kothey at Rs. 165. Deep-fried momo usually has a fried exterior; kothey commonly has a pan-fried base. Ask which preparation you are ordering, since these are different textures under one listed price.

Chilli: a sauced option

Chilli Momo is listed at Rs. 210. This style is typically tossed in a chilli-based sauce. The name does not tell you the exact heat level or sauce ingredients, so confirm both if needed.

Find the four options together in the momo category, or see other meals and snacks for the table. Portion sizes and current availability should be checked with the café.
